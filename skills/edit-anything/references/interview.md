# The mood-first interview

The interview has one job: arrive at a brief the user recognises as theirs. People rarely know the words for a look, a pace or a sound; they do know it when they see it. So show first, ask second, and translate their reactions into decisions.

One question per message. Mirror back what you heard before moving on. Label everything in `edit.json` as `seen` (with their words), `inferred` (with your basis) or `unknown`.

## 1. What it is for (purpose)

Ask, in this order, one per message:
1. **What should a viewer do or feel when it ends?** (the goal beyond the video). Done when you have one action or one feeling in their words.
2. **Who watches it?** Done when you can name the viewer and what they already know.
3. **Where does it run, and how long may it be?** Ask the platform first; propose the length from `references/routes.md` and let them confirm.

If the answer to 1 is "everything" or "more followers", ask what one viewer should do after this one video.

## 2. What already exists (material)

Ask what they have, not what they want: footage, phone takes, a recorded talk, a voice they like, a script, screenshots, a product, music, a logo and brand colours, earlier videos. Route with `references/routes.md`:
- Takes or footage of a person talking exist → route C (talking head in).
- They will be on camera but have not recorded → route D (talking head to record).
- No camera, the picture is made → route A (reel) or B (fully AI-made film), decided by length and goal.

Never assume footage exists. If they mention footage, ask for one file or a screenshot of it before you plan around it.

## 3. Mood and references (show, then ask)

1. Pick 4 to 6 references from `references/reference-library.md` that fit the goal and the route, spread over the mood axes (one calm, one energetic, one warm, one cool, one hand-made, one clean). Show them with a link or a still and one line each.
2. Ask one question: which one comes closest, and what about it? Then: which one is furthest away, and why?
3. Ask if they have their own references (a reel they saved, a channel, a film). Their own references outrank yours.
4. Translate the reactions into the five mood axes and show them for confirmation:

| Axis | Low end | High end |
|------|---------|----------|
| Energy | still, slow, breathing | punchy, fast, kinetic |
| Temperature | cool, clinical, blue-grey | warm, earthy, golden |
| Texture | clean, flat, vector | hand-made, grain, paper, brush |
| Pace | long holds, few cuts | a cut every beat |
| Type voice | quiet serif, lots of air | loud display, big and close |

Write each axis as a position (for example "energy 3 of 5") with the user's own words as the quote. A reference gives structure and timing, never its look (rule 3).

## 4. Their editor

Ask which app they want to keep editing in. If they have none: on a phone, Edits (Instagram) or CapCut; on a computer, DaVinci Resolve (free) or what they already own. See `references/handoff.md`.

## Gate 1 summary

Show the brief as a short block: goal, viewer, platform and length, route, material, the five mood axes, editor. Then the self-check and the assumptions block. End with one question: does this match what you want?

## Behaviour

- If the user wants to skip to "just make something", offer one quick route: two references, one question, then a 10-second style frame they can react to. Reacting to a frame is the fastest interview there is.
- If they ask for a look you cannot make honestly (a famous film's characters, a real person's face or voice without consent), say so in one sentence and offer the nearest honest version.
- If they mention a deadline, write it down; it decides the route more than the look does.
