# The reference library

References are shown in the interview so the user can point instead of describe. Each library below can be browsed online; pick 4 to 6 items that fit the goal and the route, spread over the mood axes, and show each with a link or a still and one line.

## How to show references

- Name what the reference is good for, not what it is ("this one keeps the camera still and lets the words do the work").
- Never show more than six at a time. Fewer, better chosen, is easier to react to.
- Show at least one that is probably wrong for them; a clear "no" teaches you as much as a "yes".
- When the user picks one, ask what about it: the colour, the pace, the type, the texture, the sound. That answer goes into the mood axes, not the reference itself.

## What a reference may give

Structure (how a reel opens, where the ask sits), timing (how long a beat holds), the kind of motion, the kind of sound. Never the look itself, a character, a logo, a typeface without a licence, or another creator's footage or music.
